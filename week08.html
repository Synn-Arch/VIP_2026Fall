01 Your ideas

Your Figma draft is a good start: it keeps the prototype's layout and adds new interventions and a cooling score. Now each feature needs a plan. Here are some points I'd like you to think about.

Your idea Things to think about
Placement rules
A precomputed no-go mask, so things can't be placed where they don't belong
  • Where is it not allowed (buildings, roads, water, existing trees)? Where is it allowed: only sidewalks?
  • Where does that data come from? Buildings and Sidewalks: OpenStreetMap? The city's open data?
  • What does the user see when they click a no-go spot? Or just users cannot place items there without seeing a warning?
Before/after view and Cooling Score
Compare the changed map with the baseline, and turn the difference into a score
  • The Layer menu already switches between Baseline, Scenario and Difference. What would your view add: a swipe slider, two maps side by side?
  • What is the score calculated from? Describe roughly how you would compute it.
Other interventions
Shading and shelters: placed like trees, with their own properties from research
  • Which ones exactly (bus shelter, sidewalk cover, ...)? Find a real example of each.
  • For trees, the user sets the height and crown radius. What can other interventions set for each new one: size, height?
  • How do you handle the analysis process? You can think about high-level strategy referring tree intervention calculation process of our current backend code.
Choosing the date
Let the user pick the exact day
  • Every date needs its own baseline. Compute them ahead of time (Then we will need to store them in somewhere), or while the user waits?
  • Any date from a calendar, or a few prepared days (a typical summer day, a heat wave)?
More ideas, or too hard?

Have another idea? Think it through the same way and bring it along. And if an idea turns out to be too hard (no data for Atlanta area that we want to implement, the model can't represent it, it takes too long to compute), cancel it and tell us why. Finding that out is a good result too.

02 My idea: a map of tiles

Here's one of mine, for the same exercise. I want to keep every analysis at 500 × 500 px (1 km × 1 km), so a run takes a few minutes instead of the 48 minutes the whole area takes. But the map should still cover the whole area. It's divided into tiles: the user clicks one, the map zooms in, and they plan interventions for that tile.

What it needs

  • A grid over the whole area (Probably city of Atlanta area)
  • A baseline for every tile, computed once, ahead of time.
  • A tile index (an outline and an id for each tile) that the map draws. Clicking a tile zooms to it and loads its baseline.
  • In the backend, each tile's rasters in their own folder, and every job says which tile it's for.
Want to build it?

If anyone wants to take this on, go for it! You will be working on this with me. I need one student who will work with me to make several baseline rasters.
